# Moment Scenario Playbook - living with people, not managing people

Use direct natural-language entry points. Always start with a useful suggestion; ask at most one or two genuine clarifiers when necessary. The source and meaning requirements in [Family Meaning Graph](family_meaning_graph.md) and [Memory-to-Experience Engine](memory_to_experience.md) still apply; do not claim these scenario ideas derive from actual NAS contents.

| Moment | User might say | Human need | Potential source anchor if explicitly approved | Useful output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Birthday | “What could make Mom's birthday personal?” | Feel seen rather than receive another product | One family recipe note or a user-told kitchen story | Ten-minute call; shared cooking; verified recipe story card |
| Graduation | “How do we celebrate our child's graduation?” | Recognize the journey and the graduate's own wishes | Selected first-day photo, school letter, family testimony | A growth-letter, favorite shared activity, photo timeline plan |
| Ordinary evening | “What small thing could I do tonight?” | Care without a calendar occasion | User-told comfort ritual | One low-pressure gesture within ten minutes |
| Gratitude | “I never properly thanked my teacher.” | Express specific impact respectfully | User's own recollection | Personal letter draft, with no unsolicited sending |
| Anniversary | “Our 15th anniversary is coming.” | Celebrate what changed and what remains | One volunteered early memory, today's preference | Revisit a ritual; new shared experience rather than staged nostalgia |
| Retirement | “How could Dad's retirement feel meaningful?” | Identity, recognition, next chapter | Selected career photo or user narrative | A voluntary story circle or craft-sharing invitation |
| Family reunion | “We haven't met for years.” | Reconnect without presuming closeness | A shared meal or place memory | Accessible gathering plan; no pressure to repair disputes |
| Long-distance care | “Grandma lives far away.” | Connection across distance | Confirmed family phrase, dish or song title | Ask for recipe by call, talk about a photo, send reviewed message draft only |
| First school day | “My child is nervous tomorrow.” | Reassurance and agency | Parent-provided experience | One empowering note; no analysis of child from images |
| New baby | “How can we welcome the baby?” | Community support without invasive gifts | User-stated family tradition | Help parents with a meal or offer childcare if requested |
| Difficult season | “We missed celebrating last year.” | Make room for grief or complications | User's own account | Gentle optional new chapter, or no action at all |
| Purposeful future | “We always said we'd make that trip.” | Carry a meaningful intention forward | User-confirmed old wish | One user-chosen Unwritten Chapter, no promise of scheduling |

## Cadence principle
- **Everyday**: one idea without NAS, 30-second/10-minute effort, no push.
- **Occasional**: small memory grounding and two options.
- **Milestone**: deeper user-selected sources, comprehensive plan and optional keepsake.
- **Later**: user may return and confirm what actually happened.

## Special cases
Never recommend a public surprise involving someone's private history without consent. Never treat a child as incapable of rejecting a celebration. Do not invent recipient's desires or psychological needs. Do not pressure strained relationships or turn bereavement into a synthetic dialogue with the deceased. No automated gift purchasing, guest outreach, background calendar checks, reminders, multimedia slideshow creation or device pairing is currently supported.
